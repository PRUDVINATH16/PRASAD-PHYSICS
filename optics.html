Optics Foundation

Wave-Particle Propagation

Light propagates through homogeneous space along rectilinear wave fronts. Radiant energy transfers instantaneously as self-sustaining electromagnetic perturbations.

c = λ · ν = 2.9979 × 10⁸ m/s

Law of Reflection

Fermat's Least Time Path

At an optical boundary, the angle of reflection equals the angle of incidence. The incident ray, reflected ray, and surface normal all reside in a single plane.

θᵢ = θᵣ  |  Coplanar Vectors

Virtual Projection

Plane Mirror Image

Reflected rays diverge from the mirror plane. Their geometric backward extrapolations intersect to form an upright, lateral-inverted, virtual image.

dₒ = dᵢ  |  Magnification m = +1.0

Curvature Optics

Principal Axis, Pole, F & C

Spherical mirrors curve with Center of Curvature (C) and Pole (P). Paraxial rays converge midway at the Principal Focus (F).

f = R / 2  |  Paraxial Limit

Concave Mirror (Case I)

Object Placed Beyond C

Incident parallel rays reflect through focus F, while rays passing through C retrace their trajectory. Image is real, inverted, and diminished.

Real, Inverted, Diminished (Between F & C)

Concave Mirror (Case II)

Object Positioned Exactly at C

Rays converge directly below the center of curvature. Height of image precisely matches height of object.

v = -2f  |  m = -1.0 (Same Size)

Concave Mirror (Case III)

Object Between F and C

As object moves nearer to the focal threshold, reflected wavefronts project further back into space, magnifying the inverted image.

Image Beyond C  |  |m| > 1.0 (Magnified)

Concave Mirror (Case IV)

Object Between Focus & Pole

Reflected rays diverge into space. The eye tracks them backward behind the mirror surface, yielding an upright, magnified virtual image.

Virtual, Erect, Magnified (Makeup / Shaving)

Convex Mirror

Wide-Angle Virtual Divergence

Convex surfaces disperse incident rays away from the principal axis. Rays appear to emerge from a focal point situated behind the mirror.

Always Virtual, Erect, Diminished  (Rear-View)

Analytical Optics

Mirror Formula & Sign Convention

Using Cartesian coordinates with Pole as origin, object distances u are negative, yielding rigorous reciprocal focal lengths.

1/f = 1/v + 1/u  |  m = -v / u = hᵢ / hₒ

Interfacial Bending

Snell's Law & Refractive Index

When light enters an optically denser medium, phase velocity decelerates, bending the wave-vector toward the normal line.

n₁ sin(θ₁) = n₂ sin(θ₂)  |  n = c / v

Refraction Dynamics

Refraction via Glass Slab

Emergent beam retains identical angular trajectory as incident ray. Interfacial bends produce a pure parallel lateral shift.

d = t · sin(θ₁ - θ₂) / cos(θ₂)

Critical Angle Boundary

Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

Past the critical angle θc, refraction into air becomes mathematically impossible; 100% of light energy reflects back internally.

sin(θ_crit) = n₂ / n₁  |  Fiber Optics

Refractive Converger

Convex Lens Geometry

Paraxial rays refract across two convex interfaces, converging with clean coherence at the secondary principal focal plane F₂.

Converging Diopter  |  Focal Length f > 0

Refractive Diverger

Concave Lens Geometry

Thinner at center than edges, incident parallel wavefronts diverge away from axis, tracing virtual paths back to primary focus F₁.

Diverging Diopter  |  Focal Length f < 0

Convex Lens (Case I)

Real & Inverted Image Formation

Object positioned outside focal length. Rays pass through the optical center (O) undeflected and intersect refracted focal rays to form a real image.

1/f = 1/v - 1/u  |  m = +v / u

Convex Lens (Case II)

Simple Magnifying Glass

Object positioned within focal point (u < f). Rays diverge after transmission; backward extensions form an erect, magnified virtual image.

Angular Magnification M = 1 + (D / f)

Lens Power

Dioptric Power & Combinations

Optical power measures lens ray-bending capacity. Thin lenses in contact add algebraically without loss of generality.

P = 1 / f (meters)  |  P_net = P₁ + P₂

Biological Optics

Myopia & Concave Correction

An elongated eyeball focuses distant light in front of the retina. A diverging concave prescription shifts the focal point onto the retina.

Myopia Corrective Power P = -1 / FarPoint

Advanced Instrument

Astronomical Refractor Telescope

Objective lens forms an inverted real image at its focal plane. Eyepiece acts as a magnifier, projecting the virtual image to infinity.

M = -fₒ / fₑ  |  Tube Length L = fₒ + fₑ
