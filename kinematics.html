Synopsis 8 // Section 1

Vertical Launch & Retardation

An object is projected upwards with initial velocity u. As it rises against Earth's gravitational pull, velocity decreases continuously until it reaches zero at maximum height.

a = -g (downwards)
v decreases continuously → v = 0 at apex

Kinematic Equation I

Velocity-Time Transformation

Substituting acceleration a = -g into standard motion equation v = u + at gives the instantaneous upward speed at any elapsed second t.

v = u - gt
Here u = u, a = -g

Kinematic Equation II

Displacement-Time Function

Substituting displacement S = h and acceleration a = -g into S = ut + ½at² models the instantaneous vertical height.

h = ut - ½gt²
Parabolic Trajectory in Space-Time

Kinematic Equation III

Velocity-Displacement Relation

Substituting a = -g and s = h into v² - u² = 2as gives a direct velocity relation independent of elapsed time.

v² - u² = -2gh
v² = u² - 2gh

Peak Elevation (Hmax)

Apex Condition & Derivation

At the highest elevation, vertical velocity ceases completely (v = 0). Solving 0² - u² = -2gH_max isolates maximum displacement.

H_max = u² / 2g
Proportional to initial kinetic energy (u²)

Time of Ascent

Duration of Upward Flight

The total duration taken to attain apex height. At apex, v = 0. Substituting into v = u - gt_a yields:

t_a = u / g
0 = u - gt_a &implies; gt_a = u

Time of Descent

Descent from Apex to Ground

Starting from rest at maximum height (u = 0) under constant downward acceleration a = +g, the body falls through height H_max.

t_d = √(2H/g) = u / g
Symmetry: t_a = t_d

Time of Flight

Combined Ascent & Descent

The net time duration the body remains airborne from the instant of projection until it strikes the ground plane.

t_f = t_a + t_d = 2u / g
Airborne Period is Doubled Ascent Time

Velocity on Reaching Ground

Symmetric Impact Velocity

Dropping from height h with u = 0 yields v = √(2gh). For vertical projection where h = u²/2g, impact speed equals launch speed.

v_impact = √(2gh) = u
Magnitude of velocity conserved upon return
